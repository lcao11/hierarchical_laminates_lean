# Reading the proofs

Read [Statements](Statements.md) first for the gradient convention, precise envelope definitions and quantified conclusions. The route below follows the manuscript: an energy equality for all hierarchical laminates, a smooth medium violating that equality, periodic two-phase realization, and the application to two quadratic branches. The formalization supplies the weak calculus and exact variational interfaces used at each stage.

## Theorem 1.1: a two-phase composite outside the lamination closure

1. **An energy equality preserved by lamination.** [MaterialData](../HierarchicalLaminates/Algebra/MaterialData.lean) specifies the rational matrices and two prescribed mean gradients. The `Algebra/` identities give an inverse-closed matrix space and positive-definite constituents. [ConstitutiveFamily](../HierarchicalLaminates/Lamination/ConstitutiveFamily.lean), [LayerEnergy](../HierarchicalLaminates/Lamination/LayerEnergy.lean) and [PhysicalLamination](../HierarchicalLaminates/Lamination/PhysicalLamination.lean) prove that every normal and proportion preserve the material family and the equality of the two effective energies. Induction and closure retain the equality for every finite hierarchy and its limits.
2. **A smooth medium that breaks the equality.** A three-cosine phase-fraction profile varies in both spatial directions, while each local tensor is that of a horizontal laminate. The `Fourier/` argument constructs the convergent reduced response and bounds its tail. [Coefficients](../HierarchicalLaminates/Finite/Coefficients.lean) checks the exact coefficient calculation: the selected energy difference vanishes through fourth order and has a positive fifth-order coefficient. The `Corrector/` and `Contrast/` proofs identify this response with the effective-energy difference. The effective tensor itself has lower-order corrections.
3. **Fine periodic stripes of the two constituents.** The `Binary/` construction freezes the profile at equally spaced heights and uses it to set each stripe width, retaining exact phase-one fraction one half. A stripwise primitive and its paired derivatives correct the oscillating flux mismatch while preserving equilibrium. The `Material/` residual estimate makes the mean-flux error tend to zero at order $N^{-1}$ for the chosen contrast. [Counterexample](../HierarchicalLaminates/Counterexample.lean) selects a finite stripe count retaining the strict energy difference.
4. **Every prescribed fraction and every larger dimension.** Horizontal layering with either pure constituent adjusts the fraction while retaining a positive energy difference. The `Composition/` recovery argument realizes periodic two-phase coefficients at exactly the prescribed fraction $0<\theta<1$. The `GClosure/` proofs supply hierarchy realization, closure inclusion and extension to every $m\ge16$ by adding uncoupled potentials with identical coefficients in both phases. [Statements/Composite](../HierarchicalLaminates/Statements/Composite.lean) assembles the quantified result.

The analytic base is in `Periodic/`, `WeakCalculus/` and `Analysis/`: weak derivatives, orthogonal projection, coercive cell problems, density and energy identification. For the attained-minimum formulation, read [Periodic/Attainment](../HierarchicalLaminates/Periodic/Attainment.lean), then [GClosure/Attainment](../HierarchicalLaminates/GClosure/Attainment.lean). These recover periodic potentials from the solver's closed gradient space and transport the minimizer to other dimensions and periods.

## Theorem 6.1: distinct envelopes for two quadratic branches

1. **Gradient energy paired with complementary energy.** The two constituents are ordered, so their pointwise energy minimum alone is one quadratic form. [Base](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/Base.lean) pairs that gradient energy with complementary energy, whose inverse tensor reverses the order, to produce two competing positive-definite homogeneous quadratic branches. [Rotation](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/Rotation.lean) gives the row-rotation and complementary-energy identities; [ExactTrials](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/ExactTrials.lean) recovers a periodic stream potential whose gradient is the rotated mean-zero part of an equilibrium flux, using the second group of sixteen potentials. [DualLamination](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/DualLamination.lean) shows that planar duality preserves the layer normal and proportions.
2. **The rank-one lower bound.** [MaterialCompactness](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/MaterialCompactness.lean) and [MinorantRegularity](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/MinorantRegularity.lean) justify compact minimization and regularity. [Minorant](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/Minorant.lean) proves rank-one convexity and the completed-square lower bound using the layer competitor inequality.
3. **The quasiconvex upper bound.** [PeriodicTrials](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/PeriodicTrials.lean) uses the same phase indicator in both cell problems. Density and energy continuity pass the trial comparison to the cell fields. The composite's positive energy difference gives a quantitative lower bound on the envelope gap. [Gap](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/Gap.lean), [Components32](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/Components32.lean) and [Statements/TwoWell](../HierarchicalLaminates/Statements/TwoWell.lean) combine the bounds on the full $2\times32$ gradient space.
4. **Weak and smooth variations.** [IntegralContinuity](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/IntegralContinuity.lean) and [H1Trial32](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/H1Trial32.lean) extend the C¹ test inequality to arbitrary weak-H¹ variations. [ExactTrials](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/ExactTrials.lean) uses an attained primal corrector and a recovered stream potential to prove the upper bound through one weak trial. [SmoothTrials](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/SmoothTrials.lean) supplies smooth-gradient density and smooth upper competitors.
5. **The envelope characterization.** Read [RegularLaminateProfile](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/RegularLaminateProfile.lean), [LaminateJensen](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/LaminateJensen.lean), [QuasiconvexRankOne](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/QuasiconvexRankOne.lean) and [EnvelopeCharacterization](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/EnvelopeCharacterization.lean) in that order. Laminate tests control the transition error; coordinate convexity gives the continuity needed for real-normal approximation; truncation handles arbitrary negative minorants. Cofinality, measurability and integrability then prove the greatest-minorant assertion for the defined envelope. The `32` companion transfers the conclusion to the full space.

For the period convention, [PeriodTransport](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/PeriodTransport.lean) and [Statements/TwoWellPeriod](../HierarchicalLaminates/Statements/TwoWellPeriod.lean) transport the nonlinear statement to every positive period.

## Further construction properties

These can be read after the main arguments:

- `Contrast/` identifies the physical cell response throughout the elliptic contrast range and proves its fifth-order asymptotic near zero. `Fourier/SmoothSynthesis` and the corrector smoothness modules turn arbitrary polynomial moments into joint smoothness.
- [MeanAll](../HierarchicalLaminates/Binary/MeanAll.lean) proves the exact half fraction for every stripe count at least two, including odd counts.
- [InverseClosure](../HierarchicalLaminates/Algebra/InverseClosure.lean), [CompactFamily](../HierarchicalLaminates/Lamination/CompactFamily.lean) and [ClosureTopology](../HierarchicalLaminates/Lamination/ClosureTopology.lean) give general inverse closure, compact material-family closure and the finite-dimensional topology bridges.
- [FullTensor](../HierarchicalLaminates/Corrector/FullTensor.lean) reconstructs the whole effective tensor and the symmetry of its reduced parameter. [RationalBranches](../HierarchicalLaminates/TwoWell/RationalBranches.lean) proves rationality of the branch matrices and the constituent energy order.
